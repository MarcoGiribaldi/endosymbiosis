Three stale facts, three silent failures

When no memory was safer than a wrong one. Same model, same three tasks, three memories. With three false facts in its memory, the AI turned them into ready-to-use commands and procedures in 3 of 3 tasks, and never warned.

Corrupted Memory, Healthy Components · Marco Giribaldi, independent researcher, Tacna, Peru · doi:10.1145/3841580.3845736 (active once the HAI 2026 proceedings are online)

Poster (PDF, English)Póster (PDF, español)
1 · It happened to us

Our notes were wrong. For ten weeks.

Our agents read shared notes when they start. Three facts in those notes were false; the oldest had been there for ten weeks. Nobody noticed, not even us.

A table prefix from before a migrationan agent used it in a backup script; we caught it by checking the real server

3 · Why it fooled us

Wrong, but convincing

Two external judges rated the answers from corrupted memory almost as useful as those from healthy memory (one gave them the same score), but they were the most overconfident: completely sure, and wrong. The honest “I don't know” looked the least useful. One false answer questioned the command but never questioned the location.

On calibration they agreed exactly on 8 of 9 answers.

It fooled us too. Not knowing which memory wrote each answer, the author (judge 1, preregistered record) marked the honest one wrong and a false one right.

Damage zone

A false note led to silent failure (3 of 3). With healthy memory or with no memory: 0 of 3. With no memory, the agent said “I don't know” or stayed general, and broke nothing. What was missing: a way to know where a fact came from and when it expires.

Why the cards are a little misleading

On purpose. Read the three fronts again: none of them looks like the wrong choice. Late at night, with a deadline and a new model, they all sound reasonable. That is how it happened to us, and it is the point of the poster: you cannot tell a wrong memory from a right one by how sure the answer sounds. Grading blind, the author himself marked the honest answer as wrong and let one of the three false ones pass.

What the game is for

The poster reports a small table: 3 of 3, 0 of 3, 0 of 3. The cards let you make the choice first and read the table afterwards, so the number on the back of your card is your own result. If you picked A, you were in good company. So were we.

How did we solve it?

We built our own protection system

The answer came much later. A note is a clue, not a fact; every fact can be traced to its source and date; a guardian blocks dangerous actions in code; a canary checks the notes at the start and end of each session; anything irreversible waits for a person's “yes”. It is open: Endosymbiosis.

This is not yet a measured result. It comes from a first exploratory test (a synthetic scenario, small samples). Its data, predictions and scripts are open on OSF: osf.io/586cq.

Limits

A small study: one model, three tasks, one answer each, a single turn, no tools. The author was judge 1 and did not know which memory he was grading. The usefulness and calibration scores are an exploratory re-evaluation after the study. It shows that this can happen, not how often.

Summaries
En español

Cuando no tener memoria fue más seguro que tener una equivocada. Mismo modelo, mismas tres tareas, tres memorias: con tres datos falsos (el 0,13 % de la memoria), la IA los convirtió en comandos y procedimientos listos para usar en 3 de 3 tareas, sin avisar nunca. Sin memoria no presentó suposiciones como hechos; con memoria sana acertó. Dos jueces externos vieron las respuestas falsas casi tan útiles como las correctas, y el «no sé» honesto como el menos útil. Nos engañó a nosotros también.
